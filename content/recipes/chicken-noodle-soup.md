pairings:
  wine:
    - Unoaked Chardonnay (Chablis) – lean and lightly buttery, matching the broth without weighing it down
    - Dry Riesling – bright acid lifts the salty stock and fresh dill or parsley
  beer:
    - Kölsch – soft and clean, it leaves the delicate broth in front
    - Belgian Blonde – gentle malt and a peppery yeast edge suit the chicken and black pepper
ingredients:
  - section: _main_
    items:
      - 2 tbsp (30 g) unsalted butter
      - 1 medium yellow onion, finely chopped
      - Salt and black pepper
      - 8 cups (2 L) chicken stock
      - 2 bay leaves or thyme sprigs, or 1 tsp (1 g) dried thyme
      - 1/4 cup (15 g) finely chopped parsley or dill, stems reserved, plus more for garnish
      - 2 medium carrots, cut into 1/2-inch (1 cm) pieces
      - 2 celery stalks, cut into 1/2-inch (1 cm) pieces
      - 6 oz (170 g) dried noodles, such as egg noodles or short pasta
      - 3 cups (420 g) shredded cooked chicken (from 1 rotisserie chicken)
steps:
  - In a large pot or Dutch oven, melt the butter over medium heat.
  - Add the onion, season with salt and pepper, and cook until softened but not browned, 5–7 minutes.
  - Add the stock, bay leaves and parsley stems and bring to a boil over high heat.
  - Add the carrots, celery and noodles and cook, uncovered, over medium-high heat until the noodles are al dente according to the package, 7–10 minutes.
  - Add the chicken and simmer just until warmed, 1–2 minutes. Pluck out the bay leaves and parsley stems, stir in the chopped parsley, and season with salt and pepper.
notes: Serves 4–6. Noodles keep absorbing broth as the soup sits; for leftovers, cook them separately and add to each bowl. See Chicken and Rice Soup for the rice version. Adapted from NYT Cooking.
